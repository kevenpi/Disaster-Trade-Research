Natural Disasters and the Composition of Exports:
Interactive Results Appendix

This appendix reports estimates from a panel of 230 exporting countries observed from 2001 to 2024, combining CEPII BACI trade flows with EM-DAT disaster records. Each estimate compares products exposed to a disaster (for example, agricultural goods after a flood) with unexposed products exported by the same country in the same year; this within-country comparison absorbs all other national conditions in that year. Value refers to export revenue in dollars, and quantity refers to physical shipments in tons; when the two diverge, prices moved. Significance is indicated in the conventional manner: *p < .05, **p < .01, ***p < .001, with † marking estimates significant only at the 10 percent level.

Notes on interpretation. All estimates are relative comparisons: they indicate that exposed products performed differently from unexposed products within the same country and year, not the change in a country's total exports. In the product-sector view, estimates are measured against machinery and electronics as the reference category, so a positive estimate can reflect a decline in the reference group rather than growth in the sector shown. Given the number of comparisons reported, isolated estimates significant only at the 10 percent level should be discounted; the conclusions of the study rest on estimates significant at the 0.1 percent level that survive robustness checks.

Method. Poisson pseudo-maximum-likelihood (PPML) regressions with exporter-year, product-year, and exporter-product fixed effects; standard errors clustered by exporter. Severe events are those in the top quartile of their disaster type by deaths or persons affected. This page is generated directly from the fitted model objects.
